ClaimClarity Schema v1

Two layers. edi is the lossless storage schema: every segment of every file, normalized at the X12 grain. analytics is the semantic layer the agent queries: seven tables shaped around claims, lines, events, reasons and deposits, with the business rules (reversals, resubmissions, what counts as a denial, payer naming) settled once in their definitions.

Why the earlier subsets were unconvincing

Both earlier "agent" layers were still shaped like the files: batch, claim, line on each side. That moved the hard part onto the agent. To answer "what's our denial rate with Aetna" it still had to know that a reversal cancels a prior payment, that a replacement 837 supersedes the original, that CO-45 is a write-off but CO-50 is a denial, and that "AETNA LIFE" on the 837 is "AETNA" on the 835. Every one of those is a place for the agent to be quietly wrong.

A semantic layer earns its place by deciding those things once. The test for each table below is: can the agent answer its question with a WHERE and a GROUP BY, without knowing any X12 rules?

How it's built

  • claims, claim_lines, claim_events and adjustments are materialized to Parquet after each load. They need window logic over versions and reversals, and the agent should never wait on that.
  • deposits, deposit_adjustments and payers are plain DuckDB views over edi.
  • Two small curated tables feed the rules: edi.payer_alias (837 and 835 payer names and IDs → one payer) and edi.carc_category (each CARC → contractual, denial, patient responsibility, other payer, other; seeded from the 23 reviewed denial CARCs).
  • Layout per tenant: tenants/labcorp/edi/… and tenants/labcorp/analytics/…. The agent's catalog only attaches analytics.

Open decisions

  • Claim grain. A claim here is one PCN sent to one payer. A PCN billed to primary then secondary is two claims. Confirm that matches how the implementations team talks about claims.
  • Denial definition. Reuses the current rule (recorded CLP02 = 4, or the v3 signal). If the team changes it, only edi.carc_category and two view definitions change.
  • Contractual list. Which CO reasons count as normal write-offs rather than denials (45, 253 to start) needs an owner.
  • Payer aliases. Someone has to curate payer_alias per tenant; the existing payer resolver could propose matches for review.
